\begin{abstract}
Quantum-gravitational spacetime foam imprints an achromatic, stochastic
arrival-time jitter on light from distant sources. We show that this jitter
modifies the first-order temporal coherence $g^{(1)}(\tau)$ and hence the
observed spectrum, providing a spectroscopic probe complementary to existing
imaging-channel (PSF, fringe-visibility, Strehl) constraints. In the
narrow-band limit the spectrum factorizes into a coherent line core suppressed
by $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$ and an incoherent pedestal, yielding a spectral
shape-distortion bound on the Ng--Perlman foam exponent $\alpha$.
In the dynamical-foam limit ($\tau_{\rm foam}\ll\tau_c$), applied to the
MeV emission lines of GRB\,221009A we obtain $\alpha \gtrsim 0.64$--$0.65$
(dynamical and quasi-static brackets, respectively).
For the broadband CMB the foam induces an $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}$ deficit
($\sigma_\phi^2 \propto \nu^2$) with a low-frequency pedestal; a Fisher
analysis of COBE/FIRAS reaches $\alpha \lesssim 0.57$ under the placeholder
$K_\nu^{\rm fast}\propto 1/\nu$ pedestal kernel (modulo a
$\Delta\alpha\sim 0.03$ cosmological-redshift systematic); the
cosmological-accumulation variant tightens the FIRAS threshold to
$\alpha\lesssim 0.60$, with PIXIE projected to reach
$\alpha\lesssim 0.59$ from instrumental noise improvement alone.  The final FIRAS threshold awaits
a first-principles derivation of the foam temporal autocorrelation
$C_\alpha(\tau)$, which determines the pedestal shape and hence the
foam--$\mu$ degeneracy that currently dominates the error budget. None of these bounds exclude
the holographic value $\alpha = 2/3$, which would require a confirmed
narrow spectral line at $E_{\rm obs} \gtrsim 720$\,MeV in a $z \sim 0.15$ GRB.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A long-standing expectation from the union of quantum mechanics and general
relativity is that the smooth pseudo-Riemannian manifold of classical spacetime
breaks down at the Planck length
$\ell_{\rm P} = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3} \simeq 1.616\times 10^{-35}$~m.
In Wheeler's original ``spacetime foam'' picture~\cite{Wheeler1955}, quantum
fluctuations of the metric on this scale render the geometry stochastic;
similar conclusions are reached in the Euclidean path-integral
treatment~\cite{Hawking1978} and in modern holographic and entropic-gravity
arguments from very different starting points.
While a complete theory of quantum gravity remains elusive, the question of
whether residual imprints of Planck-scale geometric fluctuations can accumulate
over cosmological baselines and become observable is one of the few empirical
handles on physics in the Planck regime, and it has motivated a substantial
body of phenomenological work over the past two
decades~\cite{Carlip2023,NgPerlman2022}.

A widely studied parametrization due to Ng and
collaborators~\cite{Ng2001,Ng2003,NgPerlman2022}, the $\alpha$-foam model,
encodes the foam dynamics in a power-law path-length variance
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\ell^2(D) = A_\alpha\,D^{2(1-\alpha)}\,\ell_{\rm P}^{2\alpha},
  \label{eq:NgPerlmanVar}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is the proper distance to the source, $\alpha$ parametrizes the foam
model, and $A_\alpha$ is a dimensionless coefficient of order unity.
A photon traversing distance $D$ passes through approximately
$N \sim D/\ell_{\rm P}$ independent Planck-scale spacetime cells, each imparting
a tiny random displacement to the photon path; the exponent $\alpha$ controls
how the per-cell displacements accumulate: for random-walk foam ($\alpha=1/2$),
contributions add in quadrature ($\sigma_\ell \propto \sqrt{D\ell_{\rm P}}$);
holographic foam ($\alpha=2/3$) grows more slowly, constrained by an area-law
bound on the number of independent degrees of freedom per Planck area; and
conservative foam ($\alpha=1$) yields a distance-independent
$\sigma_\ell \propto \ell_{\rm P}$, corresponding to perfectly correlated cell
contributions.

The primary observational probe of stochastic foam to date has been the imaging
channel. Spacetime foam accumulates a cumulative phase dispersion
$\Delta\phi \simeq 2\pi\ell^{1-\alpha}\ell_{\rm P}^{\alpha}/\lambda$ across the
entrance aperture, degrading PSF sharpness and erasing interference fringes once
$\Delta\phi \gtrsim 2\pi$~\cite{LieuHillman2003}.
For PKS\,1413$+$135 ($\ell = 1.216$~Gpc) observed with HST at $\lambda=1.6\,\mu$m,
the random-walk model ($\alpha=1/2$) predicts $\Delta\phi \sim 10\times 2\pi$,
so the detection of Airy rings in this image marginally rules out that model;
the holographic model ($\alpha=2/3$) predicts $\Delta\phi \sim 10^{-9}\times 2\pi$
and is obviously not ruled out~\cite{NgPerlman2022,Christiansen2011,Perlman2015}.
Models with long-range transverse correlations evade even the random-walk
bound~\cite{LeeZurekChen2024}.

The most luminous GRB ever recorded, GRB\,221009A at $z=0.151$, has been
examined in the imaging channel by Steinbring~\cite{Steinbring2023} and
Steinbring \& Ng~\cite{SteinbringNg2026}.
Using a multiwavelength average of foam-induced angular broadening ---
analogous to a seeing disk from atmospheric turbulence ---
Steinbring~\cite{Steinbring2023} showed that the holographic ($\alpha=2/3$)
model predicts an angular widening below $\sim\!1^{\circ}$, consistent
with the Fermi and Swift localization data for GRBs at $z\leq 5$ up to
peak energies of $\sim\!10\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
Steinbring \& Ng~\cite{SteinbringNg2026} extended this to the full
multiwavelength follow-up of GRB\,221009A --- from optical and near-infrared
through X-rays and the highest-energy gamma-rays ever attributed to a GRB ---
computing the holographic-foam-widened PSF for real telescopes and finding all
data consistent with $\alpha=2/3$.
These imaging-channel analyses show that the holographic model is not
excluded by the spatial localization of GRB\,221009A.

We develop a framework based on the first-order temporal coherence
function $g^{(1)}(\tau)$ of light from astrophysical sources.
This function is complementary to the first-order spatial coherence
function $g^{(1)}(B)$, which forms the basis of imaging-channel probes.
The first-order coherence --- the field--field autocorrelation ---
inherits a foam suppression factor
that decreases continuously from unity toward complete fringe erasure as
the accumulated phase variance $\sigma_\phi^2$ grows.
In our framework the phase variance arises from temporal fluctuations of
foam, whereas in the imaging channel it arises from spatial fluctuations;
correspondingly, foam imprints itself on the spectrum rather than on the
spatial brightness profile.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} introduces the spacetime foam model: the
arrival-time jitter parameterization, the temporal autocorrelation
$C_\alpha(\tau)$, and the distribution of differential jitter.
Section~\ref{sec:g1} derives the first-order coherence function
$g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau)$ of the observed field, establishes closed-form
the Gaussian closed form, and identifies the narrow-band
limit in which the foam suppression factor simplifies.
Section~\ref{sec:spec} translates these results into spectroscopic observables
via the Wiener--Khinchin theorem and connects them to fringe visibility and the
spectral Strehl ratio.
Section~\ref{sec:sources} applies the spectral shape-distortion bound to two sources:
GRB\,221009A (Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a}) and the CMB as observed by
COBE/FIRAS (Section~\ref{sec:CMB}).
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} summarizes the main results and outlines future
observational directions.

\section{Spacetime Foam Model}
\label{sec:model}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Arrival-Time Jitter as a Time Reparameterization}

We model the detected field as a stochastically time-warped version of the
intrinsic source field:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm obs}(t) = E_{\rm src}\!\left(t - \delta t(t)\right),
  \label{eq:Eobs}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\rm src}(t)$ is a single spatial mode of a stationary zero-mean
complex Gaussian process (chaotic light, appropriate for the thermal and incoherent
non-thermal sources considered here, including GRB high-energy
emission and the CMB) and $\delta t(t)$ is the foam-induced
arrival-time jitter, a stationary stochastic process statistically independent
of $E_{\rm src}$.
The jitter arises from cumulative path-length fluctuations along the
line of sight of propagation distance $D$:
\begin{equation}
  \delta t(t) = \frac{1}{c}\,\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t).
  \label{eq:jitter}
\end{equation}
The vacuum $c$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:jitter} reflects the standard
Ng--Perlman treatment of foam as a small perturbation atop a flat
(or FLRW) background, with photons travelling at $c$ along
stochastically perturbed geodesics.
In the geometric-optics limit the path-length shift $\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}$
is a purely geometric quantity and hence frequency-independent (achromatic),
in contrast to plasma dispersion which scales as $\lambda^2$.
This achromaticity distinguishes foam from other astrophysical screens
--- such as plasma dispersion, interstellar scintillation, and atmospheric
turbulence --- all of which imprint frequency-dependent signatures.
For broadband sources such as the CMB, the foam distortion is likewise
distinct from standard spectral distortions ($\mu$, $y$) that have
different frequency dependences. 

\subsection{Temporal Decorrelation of Spacetime Foam}
\label{sec:Calpha_origin}

The scalar variance $\sigma_\ell^2(D)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar})
characterizes the amplitude of foam-induced path-length fluctuations along
a single ray; here we describe how those fluctuations are correlated
across time (temporal decorrelation).

The temporal evolution of spacetime foam --- an aspect not specified by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar} --- determines how the arrival-time jitter
$\delta t(t)$ along a single line of sight is correlated across the lag $\tau$.
Quantum foam models posit that each Planck-scale cell along the line of sight
fluctuates on a characteristic timescale $\tau_{\rm foam}$, so that the jitter
at times $t$ and $t+\tau$ is correlated for $\tau \ll \tau_{\rm foam}$ and
essentially independent for $\tau \gg \tau_{\rm foam}$.
We introduce $C_\alpha(\tau;D)$ as the normalized \emph{temporal}
autocorrelation of the integrated path-length fluctuation
$\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t)$, i.e.\ $C_\alpha(\tau;D) \equiv
\langle\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t)\,\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t+\tau)\rangle
/\sigma_\ell^2(D)$, which inherits the temporal structure of the
per-cell foam dynamics over the $\sim D/\ell_{\rm P}$ cells crossed by
the line of sight.
Because $C_\alpha$ is defined on the \emph{integrated} path, its
temporal shape inherits a residual dependence on $D$ from the way
per-cell foam dynamics combine along the line of sight; the explicit
$D$-argument records this dependence.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
  \big\langle \Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t)\,
              \Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t+\tau) \big\rangle
  = \sigma_\ell^2(D)\,C_\alpha(\tau;D).
  \label{eq:pathcov1ray}
\end{equation}

The timescale $\tau_{\rm foam}$ is an additional free parameter of the
foam model, not fixed by the $\alpha$-model variance relation alone.
In the standard phenomenological framework~\cite{AmelinoCamelia2013,Ng2003},
each of the $\sim D/\ell_P$ foam cells along the line of sight
independently adopts a new configuration on timescale
$\tau_P \equiv \ell_P/c \approx 5\times10^{-44}$~s; the integrated
path-length jitter $\delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t)$ --- a sum of
$\sim D/\ell_P$ independent contributions --- likewise decorrelates on
timescale $\tau_{\rm foam}\sim\tau_P$.  With $C_\alpha(\tau)\approx 0$
for all nonzero accessible lags, the foam is fully decorrelated on any
observable timescale --- the most optimistic scenario for detection ---
and for any astrophysical source considered here
($\tau_c/\tau_{\rm foam}\gtrsim 10^{22}$ even at $37$\,MeV) the
analysis sits deep within the dynamical regime.  Models with long-range
\emph{temporal} correlations --- the temporal analogue of the
long-range \emph{transverse} spatial correlations of
Ref.~\cite{LeeZurekChen2024} that evade interferometric imaging bounds
--- would give $C_\alpha(\tau)\approx 1$ over macroscopic timescales
and thereby drive $\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\to 0$, suppressing
\emph{both} the GRB spectroscopic signal of
Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a} and the CMB spectral distortion of
Section~\ref{sec:CMB}.  The two channels therefore probe the same temporal
decorrelation regime, and both depend on the dynamical-foam assumption
$\tau_{\rm foam}\ll\tau_c$ adopted here.

\subsection{Differential Jitter and Its Distribution}
\label{sec:differential_jitter}

With the temporal covariance model in place, we define the key derived quantity
that enters the coherence integrals.
Define the differential jitter between photons arriving at
times $t$ and $t+\tau$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t(\tau)
  \equiv \delta t(t+\tau) - \delta t(t).
  \label{eq:DeltaExt}
\end{equation}
For fixed $D$, stationarity implies that the distribution of
$\Delta_t(\tau)$ depends only on $\tau$; we suppress the $t$
subscript and write $\Delta(\tau)$ henceforth.
Expanding the square and applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:pathcov1ray}:
\begin{align}
  \sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)
  &= \frac{1}{c^2}\big\langle
    [\Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t+\tau)
     - \Delta\ell_{\rm tot}(t)]^2
    \big\rangle \notag \\
  &= \frac{1}{c^2}\left[
    \sigma_\ell^2 + \sigma_\ell^2
    - 2\sigma_\ell^2\,C_\alpha(\tau)
    \right] \notag \\[4pt]
  &= \frac{2\sigma_\ell^2(D)}{c^2}
    \left[1 - C_\alpha(\tau;D)\right].
  \label{eq:diffVarExt}
\end{align}

By the central limit theorem (CLT), $\Delta(\tau)$
is a sum of contributions from many foam cells and is therefore Gaussian with zero mean:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(\tau)
  \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\,\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\right),
  \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_\Delta^2$ is taken from Eq.~\eqref{eq:diffVarExt}; for
any source at $D \gtrsim 1$~kpc, the cell count
$N_{\rm cells} \sim D/\ell_{\rm P} \gtrsim 10^{54}$ amply satisfies
the large-$N$ requirement and higher-cumulant corrections are negligible.  For $\alpha = 1/2$ (random walk), per-cell path-length variances
are equal ($\ell_{\rm P}^2$ each) and the CLT applies in its standard form.
For $\alpha \neq 1/2$, the per-cell contributions scale differently with $D$
and the equality of per-cell variances is a model assumption rather than a
direct CLT consequence; Gaussianity is adopted as the minimal assumption
consistent with the $\alpha$-model.

The characteristic function of $\Delta(\tau)$ at carrier frequency
$\omega_0$ defines a foam suppression factor applicable to any source
profile.  Since $\Delta(\tau)$ is Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
  \big\langle e^{i\omega_0\Delta(\tau)}\big\rangle_{\delta t}
  = e^{-\omega_0^2\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)/2}
  \;\equiv\; \mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau),
  \label{eq:gaussian_jitter}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(0) = 1$.

From the foam path-length variance $\sigma_\ell^2(D_C)$ and carrier
frequency $\omega_0$, we define $\sigma_\phi^2 \equiv
\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_0^2\cdot(2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2) = (\omega_0\sigma_\ell/c)^2$
as the asymptotic phase variance --- the value
$\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_0^2\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)$ approaches as
$C_\alpha(\tau;D)\to 0$.  In the dynamical-foam limit, the
Ng--Perlman path-length model~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar} with $D_C$ the
line-of-sight comoving distance~\cite{NgPerlman2022} gives
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\phi^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{\rm obs}}\right)^{\!2}
    A_\alpha\,D_C^{2(1-\alpha)}\,\ell_P^{2\alpha}.
  \label{eq:sigmaphi}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau) =
(2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2)[1-C_\alpha(\tau;D)]$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:diffVarExt}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian_jitter} takes the compact form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau) = e^{-\sigma_\phi^2[1-C_\alpha(\tau;D)]}.
  \label{eq:Ffoam}
\end{equation}
At $\tau\to\infty$: $\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\infty)$ equals
$e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$ in the dynamical limit ($C_\alpha\to 0$) and
approaches unity for quasi-static foam ($C_\alpha\approx 1$).
Equation~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi} uses observer-frame wavelength
$\lambda_{\rm obs}$ throughout and so neglects the cosmological
redshift of the photon along the propagation path: at redshift $z'$
the photon's local wavelength was $\lambda(z') = \lambda_{\rm obs}/(1+z')$,
and the phase accumulated per unit path length scales as $1/\lambda^2$.
For a model in which foam path-length variance accumulates uniformly in
comoving distance, the redshift-weighted form is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\phi^2
  \;=\; \left(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{\rm obs}}\right)^{\!2}
        \int_0^{z_{\rm s}}\!(1+z')^2\,\frac{d\sigma_\ell^2}{dD_C}\,
        \frac{dD_C}{dz'}\,dz'\,,
  \label{eq:sigmaphi_cosmo}
\end{equation}
which reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi} when $(1+z')^2$ can be taken
constant over the path.  For the GRB at $z=0.151$ the path-averaged
$\langle(1+z')^2\rangle_{D_C}\approx 1.16$, enhancing $\sigma_\phi^2$
by $16\%$ --- negligible at the precision of the present analysis.
For the CMB at $z_{\rm LS}\approx 1089$ the weighting is dominated by
the high-$z$ portion of the path: with Planck~2018 flat $\Lambda$CDM
the size and sign of this correction depend on how foam fluctuations
evolve in cosmic time; two natural anchorings bracket the range:
(a)~variance accumulating uniformly in comoving distance,
$d\sigma_\ell^2/dD_C = \text{const}$, enhances $\sigma_\phi^2$ by a
factor of $\sim 10^4$;
(b)~variance anchored to proper-now coordinates with foam frozen at
the present epoch gives $(1+z')^2\to 1$ inside the integral,
leaving $\sigma_\phi^2$ unchanged.
Bounds quoted below use Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi} with $A_\alpha=1$ and
adopt anchoring (b); the CMB systematic of Section~\ref{sec:CMB}
discusses the corresponding shift under anchoring (a).

\section{First-Order Coherence }
\label{sec:g1}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Jitter Convolution Formula for $g^{(1)}$}

The observed first-order coherence is
\begin{equation}
  g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau) = \frac{\langle E^*_{\rm obs}(t)\,E_{\rm obs}(t+\tau)\rangle}
                                  {\langle|E_{\rm obs}(t)|^2\rangle}.
  \label{eq:g1def}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:Eobs} and letting
$u = t - \delta t(t)$, $v = t+\tau - \delta t(t+\tau)$, so that
$v - u = \tau - \Delta(\tau)$, stationarity of $E_{\rm src}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \langle E^*_{\rm src}(u)\,E_{\rm src}(v)\rangle
  = \langle|E_{\rm src}|^2\rangle\,g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau - \Delta(\tau)).
\end{equation}
Since $\delta t$ is independent of $E_{\rm src}$, and since
$\langle|E_{\rm obs}(t)|^2\rangle = \langle|E_{\rm src}(t-\delta t)|^2\rangle
= \langle|E_{\rm src}|^2\rangle$ by stationarity (so the normalisation
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1def} cancels), averaging over the jitter yields
\begin{equation}
  g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau)
    = \big\langle g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau - \Delta(\tau))\big\rangle_{\delta t}
    = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathcal{N}(\Delta;0,\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau))\,
      g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau - \Delta)\,d\Delta.
  \label{eq:g1obs}
\end{equation}
The observed first-order coherence is the intrinsic coherence function
convolved with the Gaussian differential-jitter kernel.

\subsection{Gaussian Source Profile}
\label{sec:closedforms}

The GRB\,221009A emission line is well described by a Gaussian profile
in each epoch~\cite{YQZhang2024}, and it serves as the representative
case admitting the simplest closed form.
A Gaussian line of $1\sigma$ width $\sigma_\nu$ centred on $\nu_0$
corresponds via the Wiener--Khinchin theorem to the coherence function
\begin{equation}
  g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau) \;=\; e^{-i\omega_0\tau}\,e^{-\tau^2/2\tau_G^2},
  \label{eq:g1src_gauss}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_0 \equiv 2\pi\nu_0$ and $\tau_G \equiv 1/(2\pi\sigma_\nu)$ is the
Gaussian coherence decay time.  The carrier factor $e^{-i\omega_0\tau}$
encodes the line's position in the spectrum and must be retained: dropping
it would substitute a baseband Gaussian centred at $\nu=0$ for the physical
line and would lose the dominant foam effect (line-peak suppression).
Substituting into the jitter-convolution integral~\eqref{eq:g1obs} and
completing the square gives the exact closed form
\begin{align}
  g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau)
  &= \frac{\tau_G}{\sqrt{\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)}}\,
     \exp\!\left[\,-\,i\,\omega_0\tau\,
       \frac{\tau_G^2}{\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)}\right] \notag\\
  &\quad\times \exp\!\left[\,-\,\frac{\tau^2+\omega_0^2\,
       \tau_G^2\,\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)}
       {2\bigl[\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\bigr]}\right].
  \label{eq:g1obs_gauss}
\end{align}
The observed coherence is again a Gaussian-modulated oscillation, but
with three foam-induced modifications: (i) a slight redshift of the
carrier, $\nu_0^{\rm eff} = \nu_0\,\tau_G^2/[\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)]$;
(ii) a wider Gaussian decay of $g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau)$ in lag (decay
scale $\sqrt{\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)}$ vs.\ $\tau_G$);
and (iii) a global amplitude suppression by the factor
$\exp\!\left[-\omega_0^2\tau_G^2\sigma_\Delta^2/\{2(\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2)\}\right]$.
Normalisation $g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(0)=1$ holds because $\sigma_\Delta(0)=0$.

For the Gaussian source, $\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau)$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ffoam} matches the item-(iii) amplitude suppression
only in the narrow-band limit $\sigma_\Delta\ll\tau_G$; otherwise
the full Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} must be used.

\section{Implications for Spectroscopy}
\label{sec:spec}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{The Wiener--Khinchin Bridge}

The foam-modified observed coherence function — given in general by the
jitter-convolution integral Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs} with closed forms
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} — carries direct
spectroscopic content: for a stationary field the power spectral density
$S(\nu)$ and $g^{(1)}(\tau)$ are Fourier-transform pairs,
implying
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm obs}(\nu) = \mathcal{F}\!\left\{g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau)\right\}(\nu).
  \label{eq:WK_obs}
\end{equation}
The theorem applies because astronomical emission lines have finite
linewidth (so the Fourier transform is well defined) and because the
foam-induced jitter is a stationary process independent of the source
(foam acts on photons after emission, while $\delta t(t)$ statistics are
time-translation invariant on observational timescales).  The observed
field is then stationary when the integration time exceeds the foam
coherence time $\tau_{\rm foam}$ (the lag scale beyond which
$C_\alpha(\tau;D)\to 0$), so that the time-averaged spectrum coincides
with its ensemble average: $T_{\rm int}\gg\tau_{\rm foam}$.
In the weak-jitter and narrow-band regimes the jitter-convolution
integral~\eqref{eq:g1obs} reduces to the lag-domain product
\begin{equation}
  g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}(\tau) = g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau)\,\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau),
  \label{eq:g1_product}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau) = \exp\{-\sigma_\phi^2[1-C_\alpha(\tau;D)]\}$
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ffoam}.  Applying the convolution theorem to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:WK_obs} then yields
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm obs}(\nu) = S_{\rm src}(\nu)*\mathcal{K}_{\rm foam}(\nu),
  \qquad \mathcal{K}_{\rm foam}(\nu) \equiv \mathcal{F}\{\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}\}(\nu),
  \label{eq:Sobs_conv}
\end{equation}
a convolution by a foam kernel that depends on $\sigma_\phi^2$,
$C_\alpha$, and $\tau_{\rm foam}$.

For the Gaussian source profile of Section~\ref{sec:closedforms},
Fourier-transforming Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} gives a Gaussian line
centred on $\nu_0^{\rm eff}$ with width
$\sigma_{\rm obs} = 1/[2\pi\sqrt{\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2}]$ —
\emph{narrower} than $\sigma_\nu$ (wider $g^{(1)}$-support in $\tau$
maps to narrower $S$-support in $\nu$) — and with peak amplitude
suppressed by the factor in item~(iii) of
Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}.  In the dynamical limit where
$\sigma_\Delta^2$ is constant in $\tau$, the lag-domain
convolution~\eqref{eq:g1obs} corresponds by the convolution theorem to a
\emph{multiplication} of the spectrum by a Gaussian factor centred on
$\nu=0$, which attenuates and shifts the line rather than convolving
it in frequency.

In the narrow-band limit $\sigma_\Delta\ll\tau_G$ the width change and
carrier shift become negligible and peak suppression dominates,
giving
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm obs}(\nu_0) \;=\; e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}\,S_{\rm src}(\nu_0),
  \qquad
  \sigma_\phi^2 \;\equiv\; \tfrac{1}{2}\omega_0^2\sigma_\Delta^2.
  \label{eq:sigma_quadrature}
\end{equation}
The definition $\sigma_\phi^2 \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}\omega_0^2\sigma_\Delta^2$
in the dynamical-foam limit, where $\sigma_\Delta^2\to 2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2$, gives
$\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_0^2\sigma_\Delta^2 = (2\pi\sigma_\ell/\lambda_{\rm obs})^2$
matching Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi}.

In the further dynamical limit $C_\alpha(\tau)\to 0$ as
$|\tau|\to\infty$, $\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau)$ acquires a nonzero
asymptote $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$, and the kernel decomposes as
$\mathcal{K}_{\rm foam}(\nu) = e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}\delta(\nu) +
\mathcal{K}_{\rm ped}(\nu)$.  The observed spectrum then splits into
a coherent core $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}S_{\rm src}(\nu)$ and a broad
pedestal carrying the complementary fraction $1-e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$
of the source flux.  For the Gaussian source this split is transparent
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss}: the coherent core is governed by the
asymptotic value $\sigma_\Delta^2\to 2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2$ for
$|\tau|\gtrsim\tau_{\rm foam}$, while the pedestal arises from the
variation of $\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)$ at $|\tau|\lesssim\tau_{\rm foam}$.
The broadband CMB derivation in Section~\ref{sec:CMB_derivation}
treats this decomposition explicitly.

\subsection{Connection to Fringe Visibility and Strehl Ratio}
\label{sec:connection}

The first-order coherence function $g^{(1)}_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau)$
derived above describes \emph{temporal} coherence along a single line
of sight, with the foam suppression governed by the temporal
decorrelation $C_\alpha(\tau;D)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ffoam}. The fringe visibility
and Strehl ratio are the \emph{spatial} counterparts: they probe phase
variations between two points in the telescope aperture plane
separated by a baseline $B$, with the suppression governed by an
aperture-plane coherence function $C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(B;D)$
(constructed by direct analogy to the temporal $C_\alpha(\tau;D)$ of
Section~\ref{sec:Calpha_origin}, under the same foam-cell statistics).

The structural equivalence between the temporal and spatial cases is
not merely formal: in both, the foam suppression arises as the
characteristic function of a zero-mean Gaussian differential random
variable, with $C_\alpha$ (respectively $C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}$)
setting how that variable's variance grows with separation.
Temporally, the relevant random variable is the differential jitter
$\Delta(\tau) = \delta t(t+\tau) - \delta t(t)$ between two arrival
times along a single ray, with variance
$\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau) = (2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2)[1-C_\alpha(\tau;D)]$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:diffVarExt}). Spatially, the analogous random variable is the
differential phase
$\Delta\phi(B) = \phi(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{B}) - \phi(\mathbf{x})$
between two points on the aperture plane separated by baseline $B$,
with variance
%
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\Delta\phi}^2(B) \;=\; 2\sigma_\phi^2\bigl[1-C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(B;D)\bigr].
  \label{eq:dphi-variance}
\end{equation}
%
Applying the Gaussian characteristic-function identity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian_jitter} in
each domain yields suppression factors of identical form---one
expressed in lag, the other in baseline:
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sp}}_{\mathrm{foam}}(B)
   \;\equiv\; \exp\!\bigl\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_{\Delta\phi}^2(B)\bigr\}
   \;=\; \exp\!\bigl\{-\sigma_\phi^2\bigl[1-C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(B;D)\bigr]\bigr\},
  \label{eq:Fspat}
\end{equation}
%
mirroring $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{foam}}(\tau)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ffoam} under
$C_\alpha(\tau;D) \leftrightarrow C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(B;D)$. The
shared subscript $\alpha$ further reflects that the same cumulative
growth law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar} underlies both decorrelations. As with its
temporal counterpart, $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sp}}_{\mathrm{foam}}$ runs
from unity at zero baseline ($C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(0;D)=1$, the two
points sample identically phased wavefronts) to maximum suppression
$e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$ in the resolved limit where
$C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}} \to 0$ (the two points sample independent
foam realizations).

The two-element interferometric fringe visibility at baseline $B$ is
then
%
\begin{equation}
  V(B) \;=\; V_{\mathrm{src}}(B)\,\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sp}}_{\mathrm{foam}}(B)
       \;=\; V_{\mathrm{src}}(B)\,\exp\!\bigl\{-\sigma_\phi^2\bigl[1-C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(B;D)\bigr]\bigr\},
  \label{eq:visibility}
\end{equation}
%
where $V_{\mathrm{src}}(B)=|g^{(1)}_{\mathrm{src}}(B)|$ denotes the
source's first-order \emph{spatial} coherence at baseline $B$ --- the
van Cittert--Zernike Fourier transform of the source brightness
distribution at spatial frequency $B/\lambda$, distinct from the
temporal $g^{(1)}_{\rm src}(\tau)$ used elsewhere in this paper. The Strehl ratio of a single aperture of
diameter $D_{\mathrm{tel}}$ is the same statistic with $B$ replaced by
the internal aperture baseline: when
$C_\alpha^{\mathrm{sp}}(D_{\mathrm{tel}};D) \to 0$ the aperture-plane
phases are independent draws from the single-ray distribution and the
Gaussian-phase (extended Mar\'echal) form gives~\cite{NgPerlman2022}
%
\begin{equation}
  S \;\simeq\; e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}.
  \label{eq:strehl}
\end{equation}
%
Strehl ratio and fringe visibility therefore measure the \emph{same}
underlying phase variance $\sigma_\phi^2 = (2\pi\sigma_\ell/\lambda)^2$
in the resolved limit, differing only in which part of the point-spread-function is being considered.
This unification places the published imaging
constraints~\cite{NgPerlman2022,LieuHillman2003,Christiansen2011,Perlman2015}
on a common footing within the present framework.

Equation~\eqref{eq:visibility} also reveals a Fourier duality between
the imaging constraints of Lieu \& Hillman~\cite{LieuHillman2003} and
Ng \& Perlman~\cite{NgPerlman2022} and the spectroscopic framework
developed here.  In imaging, the aperture-plane field Fourier-transforms
to the point-spread function; foam-induced \emph{spatial} decoherence
across the aperture, encoded in $C_\alpha^{\rm sp}(B;D)$, perturbs the
PSF as Strehl loss and fringe washout.  In spectroscopy, the first-order
coherence $g^{(1)}(\tau)$ Fourier-transforms to the spectrum
(Wiener--Khinchin); foam-induced \emph{temporal} decoherence in lag,
encoded in $C_\alpha(\tau;D)$, perturbs the spectrum as line-peak
suppression and a broad pedestal.  Both channels probe the same
single-point phase variance $\sigma_\phi^2 = (2\pi\sigma_\ell/\lambda)^2$
through conjugate Fourier variables (aperture $\leftrightarrow$ lag,
angle $\leftrightarrow$ frequency), with the rapid chromatic tightening
$\sigma_\phi \propto 1/\lambda$ exploited in both Ng \&
Perlman's~\cite{NgPerlman2022} Table~1 and the analyses of
Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a}.  The duality is not perfect at the
observational interface: isolating the foam contribution requires an
independently characterized telescope aperture in the imaging channel
and an independently characterized intrinsic source spectrum in the
spectroscopic channel.

% ==============================================================
\section{Sources}
\label{sec:sources}

Time-varying spacetime foam modifies the source first-order coherence,
and hence its spectrum, in a wavelength-dependent way through
the scaling $\sigma_\phi \propto 1/\lambda$. Two observable
consequences follow. First, individual spectral features are distorted:
since $\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)\propto\nu^2$, the foam suppression factor
$e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}$ has chromatic curvature that acts as a
Gaussian apodization across the line profile, reducing its peak --- the
spectroscopic analogue of PSF broadening in imaging. Second, the
broadband spectral energy distribution is distorted: the cosmic
microwave background, expected to be a near-perfect blackbody, would
acquire foam-induced deviations whose chromatic signature is distinct
from standard chemical-potential and Compton-$y$ distortions.
In this section we consider these two possibilities.

\subsection{GRB\,221009A}
\label{sec:grb221009a}

The Ng--Perlman imaging bound~\cite{NgPerlman2022} uses the threshold
$\sigma_\ell \lesssim \lambda$ (equivalently $\sigma_\phi\lesssim 2\pi$):
below this, the wavefront is ``coherent'' and the PSF peak survives.
For spectroscopy of a single source without an absolute flux reference,
the smooth suppression factor $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}$ suppresses line and
continuum equally at any given frequency; the equivalent width is
preserved and uniform dimming is undetectable from the spectrum alone.
A distinct shape-distortion criterion is therefore needed.

The foam redistribution scatters photon arrival times by
$\Delta t\sim\sigma_\ell/c\equiv\sigma_t$, producing spectral broadening
of order $\Delta\nu_{\rm foam}\sim c/\sigma_\ell$; this heuristic
identifies the broadening timescale with the cumulative-jitter scale
$\sigma_t$, distinct from the foam decorrelation time
$\tau_{\rm foam}$, and applies in the dynamical limit
$\sigma_t\gg\tau_{\rm foam}$.  The ratio $\sigma_t/\tau_G$ governs
where the carrier-included Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} departs from
the product form $g^{(1)}_{\rm src}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}$; the
heuristic threshold below marks the boundary
$\sigma_t\sim\tau_G$.
A spectral line of resolving power $R\equiv\nu_0/\Delta\nu_{\rm line}$
has intrinsic width $\Delta\nu_{\rm line}$.  When the foam broadening
exceeds the line width,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu_{\rm foam} \;\gtrsim\; \Delta\nu_{\rm line}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sigma_\ell \;\lesssim\; R\lambda_{\rm obs}\,,
  \label{eq:spec_strehl_thresh}
\end{equation}
the line profile is significantly distorted: its peak is reduced and its
wings broadened beyond the intrinsic shape.  (Note that the foam
redistribution bandwidth $\Delta\nu_{\rm foam}\sim c/\sigma_\ell$
decreases with increasing $\sigma_\ell$---longer jitter coherence
time---so the distortion criterion is a \emph{lower} bound on
$\sigma_\ell$.)  Defining the
\emph{spectral Strehl} $\mathcal{S}_{\rm spec}$ as the ratio of the
observed line peak to the undistorted peak:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_{\rm spec}
  \;\simeq\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + R^2\lambda_{\rm obs}^2/\sigma_\ell^2}}\,,
  \label{eq:spectral_strehl}
\end{equation}
which is the peak ratio in the heuristic picture where foam acts as a
Gaussian frequency-domain convolution kernel of bandwidth $c/\sigma_\ell$
applied to the intrinsic Gaussian line; the $\sqrt{\,}$ arises from the
ratio of peaks of two convolved Gaussians.  This approximation agrees
with the exact carrier-included form Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} to
leading order in $\sigma_\Delta/\tau_G$.  The spectral Strehl
drops to $1/\sqrt{2}$ at the threshold~\eqref{eq:spec_strehl_thresh}.
This is the spectroscopic analogue of the imaging Strehl: imaging
tests whether spatial phase variance reduces the PSF peak;
spectroscopy tests whether temporal path-length scatter broadens
the line beyond its intrinsic width.  Both are shape diagnostics
requiring no absolute calibration.

The shape-distortion threshold $\sigma_\ell \lesssim R\lambda$
permits $R$ times larger path-length jitter than the Ng--Perlman
imaging criterion $\sigma_\ell\lesssim\lambda$: a line of resolving
power $R$ tolerates jitter up to a fraction $1/R$ of its own width
before the peak falls below $1/\sqrt{2}$.
Substituting into the path-length
model~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar} and inverting (with $D_C$ the
comoving distance to the source):
\begin{equation}
  \alpha \;\gtrsim\;
  \frac{\ln(D_C/\lambda_{\rm obs}) - \ln R}{\ln(D_C/\ell_P)}\,.
  \label{eq:alpha_bound_spec}
\end{equation}
The factor $\ln(D_C/\lambda_{\rm obs})/\ln(D_C/\ell_P)$ is the
Ng--Perlman bound~\cite{NgPerlman2022} evaluated at the probe
wavelength; the correction $-\ln R/\ln(D_C/\ell_P)$ weakens it for
resolved lines ($R>1$) and vanishes for $R=1$, reflecting the weaker
threshold $\sigma_\ell\sim R\lambda$ versus the imaging criterion
$\sigma_\ell\sim\lambda$.  Because $\ln R \ll \ln(D_C/\ell_P)\approx 138$,
the correction is modest: $|\Delta\alpha| = \ln R / \ln(D_C/\ell_P)
\approx 0.02$--$0.05$ for $R = 10$--$1000$.
The spectroscopic channel yields bounds of the same form as
imaging but slightly weaker, with the compensating gain being access
to short wavelengths (MeV, X-ray) where imaging interferometry is
impossible.

GRB\,221009A (``the BOAT,'' Brightest Of All Time) is the most
energetic gamma-ray burst in the instrumental record, with isotropic-equivalent
energy $E_{\rm iso}\sim10^{55}\,\mathrm{erg}$ at redshift $z=0.151$,
a once-in-$\sim10^4$-year event~\cite{Burns2023}.
Two independent spectral analyses have reported a narrow gamma-ray
emission feature in the Fermi/GBM data, interpreted as blueshifted
electron--positron pair annihilation~\cite{Ravasio2024,YQZhang2024}, at
observed energies spanning $6$--$37\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
Rather than applying the single-line shape-distortion bound
Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_bound_spec} to individual detections, we exploit
the \emph{multi-epoch} constancy of the fractional line width across
this factor-of-six energy range to obtain a tighter constraint.
Adopting flat $\Lambda$CDM
($\Omega_M=0.27$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.73$,
$H_0=70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$), the comoving distance is
$D_C(z=0.151)\approx0.63\,\mathrm{Gpc}\approx1.93\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m}$
and $\ln(D_C/\ell_P)\approx138.3$.

\textit{Multi-epoch line data.}
Zhang et al.~\cite{YQZhang2024} perform a joint analysis of GECAM-C and
Fermi/GBM data covering the full prompt emission, including the bright
phase inaccessible to GBM alone.  They detect a Gaussian emission line
in seven time intervals during $T_{\rm ref}+246$--$360$\,s, with the
centroid evolving as $E_{\rm line}\propto t^{-1}$ from
$\approx37$\,MeV down to $\approx6$\,MeV.  In every interval the
measured ratio of line width to centroid is
$r_{\rm obs}\equiv\sigma_{\rm obs}/E_{\rm line} = 0.10\pm0.01$
($\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=4/7$; Zhang et al.\ \S4.4 and their Figure~7c),
consistent with a single constant across the full dynamic range.
This constancy is naturally explained in the pair-annihilation
interpretation: relativistic Doppler boosting from comoving frequency
$\nu'$ to observed $\nu = \mathcal{D}\nu'$ preserves the fractional
width, so the intrinsic contribution $r_{\rm int}\equiv
\sigma_{\rm int}/E$ is independent of epoch.

\textit{Width decomposition.}
The carrier-included closed form Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} gives the
observed fractional width as
\begin{equation}
  r_{\rm obs}^2(E) \;=\; r_{\rm int}^2\bigl[1+\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2(E)\bigr]
                  \;=\; r_{\rm int}^2 + r_{\rm foam}^2(E),
  \label{eq:robs_decomp}
\end{equation}
with the foam excess $r_{\rm foam}^2(E)\equiv r_{\rm int}^2\,
\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2(E)$.  Note that the relation is multiplicative
in $(1+\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2)$, not additive in independent
foam/intrinsic widths: the foam acts on the source through the
jitter-convolution Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs}, which translates to a
\emph{multiplication} of the spectrum by a Gaussian factor rather
than to a convolution with it (Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}).

The increased \emph{fractional} width
$r_{\rm obs}^2>r_{\rm int}^2$ may appear at first to contradict the
spectrum-narrowing noted after Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss}.  The two
statements refer to different ratios.  The carrier-included Eq.~%
\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} gives a slightly \emph{narrower} absolute width
($\tau_{\rm eff}^2=\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2$ in lag, hence
$\sigma_{\rm obs}<\sigma_\nu$ in frequency) but also a proportional
center shift
$\nu_0^{\rm eff}/\nu_0 = \tau_G^2/(\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2)$.  The
\emph{ratio} of width to (observed) center,
$\sigma_{\rm obs}/E_{\rm obs}
   = r_{\rm int}\,\sqrt{1+\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2}$,
nonetheless increases, and it is this fractional width that the
Zhang et al.\ analysis reports as $\sigma/E$ in each epoch.  The
form of $r_{\rm foam}^2(E)$ then depends on the temporal correlation
regime of $\delta t$.

In the \emph{quasi-static} limit ($\tau_{\rm foam}\gg\tau_c$), the
heuristic identification $\sigma_\phi=2\pi\sigma_\ell/\lambda \mapsto
\Delta\nu/\nu=\sigma_\phi/(2\pi)$ gives
\begin{equation}
  r_{\rm foam}^{\rm (qs)}(E)
  \;=\; \frac{\sigma_\ell}{\lambda_{\rm obs}(E)}
  \;=\; \frac{\sigma_\ell\,E}{hc}
  \;\propto\; E.
  \label{eq:rfoam_qs}
\end{equation}
This identification is dimensional rather than derived: the strict QS
limit of the framework of Section~\ref{sec:g1} actually predicts
\emph{no} foam imprint on the single-source line spectrum.  When
$\tau_{\rm foam}\gg\tau_c$, $\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\to 0$ for all
$|\tau|\lesssim\tau_c$ (since $C_\alpha\to 1$ over the support of
$g^{(1)}_{\rm src}$), so the jitter convolution Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs}
reduces to the identity and $g^{(1)}_{\rm obs}\approx g^{(1)}_{\rm src}$.
Physically, a frozen $\delta t$ acts as a single global time delay that
shifts the time origin but does not alter the line shape; the temporal
analogue of the spatial sampling that drives imaging-channel
constraints is unavailable to a single-line-of-sight spectroscopic
measurement.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:rfoam_qs} is therefore retained only as a
phenomenological bracket on the dynamical bound below, providing the
natural QS counterpart of the Ng--Perlman imaging-channel
$\sigma_\ell\lesssim\lambda$ scaling; it should not be read as a
framework-derived spectroscopic constraint.

In the \emph{dynamical} limit ($\tau_{\rm foam}\ll\tau_c$),
$\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\to 2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2$ for all $|\tau|>\tau_{\rm foam}$.
The dominant foam effect on the spectrum is then carrier-phase
suppression of the line peak by $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(E)}$ at unchanged
line width within the narrow-band regime $\sigma_\Delta\ll\tau_G$
(Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}).  Width broadening enters only as the
narrow-band approximation begins to fail.  Substituting
$\sigma_\Delta^2 = 2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2$ and
$\tau_G(E)=\lambda_{\rm obs}(E)/(2\pi c\,r_{\rm int})$ into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:robs_decomp} gives
$\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2(E)
   = (2\sigma_\ell^2/c^2)\cdot(2\pi c\,r_{\rm int})^2/\lambda_{\rm obs}^2(E)
   = 8\pi^2 r_{\rm int}^2\,\sigma_\ell^2/\lambda_{\rm obs}^2(E)$,
so that
$r_{\rm foam}^{({\rm dyn})}(E)
   = r_{\rm int}\sqrt{r_{\rm int}^2\cdot 8\pi^2\sigma_\ell^2/\lambda_{\rm obs}^2}
   = r_{\rm int}^2\cdot 2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\sigma_\ell/\lambda_{\rm obs}(E)$;
that is,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\rm foam}^{\rm (dyn)}(E)
  \;=\; r_{\rm int}^2\cdot\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\sigma_\ell}{\lambda_{\rm obs}(E)}
  \;=\; r_{\rm int}^2\cdot\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\sigma_\ell\,E}{hc}
  \;\propto\; E.
  \label{eq:rfoam_dyn}
\end{equation}
The corrected dynamical scaling is therefore $\propto E$, the
\emph{same} energy dependence as the quasi-static case --- and not the
$\propto E^{-1}$ scaling that follows from heuristic single-photon
time--frequency uncertainty arguments, which conflate the cumulative
path-length jitter $\sigma_\ell$ with the foam decorrelation timescale
$\tau_{\rm foam}$ that actually sets the pedestal bandwidth (see
Section~\ref{sec:CMB_derivation}).  Both regimes thus predict $r_{\rm foam}\propto E$
and admit the same two-parameter model
\begin{equation}
  r_{\rm obs}^2(E) \;=\; r_{\rm int}^2 + r_{\rm foam,0}^2\,(E/E_0)^2,
  \label{eq:robs_model}
\end{equation}
with $r_{\rm foam,0}\equiv r_{\rm foam}(E_0)$ the foam contribution at
the reference energy $E_0 = 37\,\mathrm{MeV}$
($\lambda_0 = 3.4\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$).  The QS and dynamical
cases differ only in how $r_{\rm foam,0}$ maps onto $\sigma_\ell$.

\textit{Fit and upper limit on $r_{\rm foam,0}$.}
A $\chi^2$ fit of the seven Zhang et al.\ measurements
$(E_i, r_i\pm\delta r_i)$ to the two-parameter model
Eq.~\eqref{eq:robs_model} (with $r_{\rm foam,0}\geq 0$) yields a best
fit at $r_{\rm foam,0}=0$, $r_{\rm int}=0.111$, with
$\chi^2_{\rm min}=5.3$ ($\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.88$ with 6 effective
degrees of freedom; since $r_{\rm foam,0}=0$ at the boundary the fit
reduces to one free parameter $r_{\rm int}$), confirming the
constant-$r_{\rm obs}$ hypothesis adopted by Zhang et al.\ (their
\S4.4 and Figure~7c).  Profiling over $r_{\rm int}$ gives the one-sided
95\%~CL upper limit ($\Delta\chi^2=2.71$, from the Chernoff boundary
theorem: the best fit lies on the boundary $r_{\rm foam,0}=0$, so the
LRT statistic is a 50:50 mixture of $\chi^2(1)$ and a point mass at
zero, giving the same 95th percentile as a standard one-sided Gaussian
bound)
$r_{\rm foam,0}^{\rm max} = 0.27$.  This bound applies to both the
quasi-static and dynamical regimes, which share the model
Eq.~\eqref{eq:robs_model}; the constraint is comparatively weak
because the foam signal peaks at $E_0 = 37$\,MeV, where the single
most uncertain data point (G1) resides.  With seven measurements and
per-point fractional uncertainties of $30$--$50\%$, the
$\Delta\chi^2=2.71$ coverage relies on the Gaussian-$\chi^2$
approximation; a bootstrap or profile-likelihood Monte Carlo would
refine the exact coverage but is not expected to shift the bound by
more than a few percent.

\textit{Constraint on $\alpha$.}
Converting via $\sigma_\ell^2 = D_C^{2(1-\alpha)}\ell_P^{2\alpha}$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar} with $A_\alpha=1$):
in the quasi-static case $\sigma_\ell = r_{\rm foam,0}\,\lambda_0$,
giving
\begin{equation}
  \alpha \;\gtrsim\;
  \frac{\ln(D_C/\lambda_0) + |\!\ln r_{\rm foam,0}^{\rm max}|}
       {\ln(D_C/\ell_P)}
  \;=\; \frac{89.2 + 1.3}{138.3}
  \;\approx\; 0.65\,,
  \label{eq:alpha_grb_qs}
\end{equation}
where $|\!\ln(0.27)|\approx1.3$.
In the dynamical case Eq.~\eqref{eq:rfoam_dyn} gives
$\sigma_\ell = r_{\rm foam,0}\,\lambda_0/(2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,r_{\rm int}^2)$,
so that
\begin{equation}
  \alpha \;\gtrsim\;
  \frac{\ln(D_C/\lambda_0) + |\!\ln r_{\rm foam,0}^{\rm max}|
        - |\!\ln(2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,r_{\rm int}^2)|}
       {\ln(D_C/\ell_P)}
  \;=\; \frac{89.2 + 1.3 - 2.2}{138.3}
  \;\approx\; 0.64\,,
  \label{eq:alpha_grb_dyn}
\end{equation}
where $|\!\ln(2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,r_{\rm int}^2)|\approx 2.2$ for
$r_{\rm int}=0.111$ (the argument is $<1$, so the log is negative).  The QS and dynamical $\alpha$ bounds therefore
agree to within $\Delta\alpha\sim0.01$ --- as expected, since both
share the model Eq.~\eqref{eq:robs_model} and differ only by an
$\mathcal{O}(r_{\rm int}^2)$ prefactor relating $\sigma_\ell$ to
$r_{\rm foam,0}$.  Numerically the dynamical bound is essentially
identical to that reported in earlier versions of this analysis under
the (incorrect) $\propto 1/E$ scaling, but the derivation no longer
relies on conflating $\sigma_t=\sigma_\ell/c$ with $\tau_{\rm foam}$.

For comparison, the single-line spectral shape-distortion bound
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_bound_spec} with $R\approx10$) applied to the
same 37\,MeV detection gives $\alpha\gtrsim(89.2-2.3)/138.3\approx0.63$.
The multi-epoch lever arm improves the bound by
$\Delta\alpha\approx0.01$--$0.02$, the improvement being modest because
the individual line-width measurements carry $30$--$50\%$ fractional
uncertainties.

\textit{Caveats.}
(i)~The G1 interval ($T+246$--$256$\,s, $E\approx37$\,MeV) lies at or
beyond the upper boundary of the GBM BGO detector range
($4$--$35$\,MeV), so its line-width measurement carries larger
systematic uncertainty than the later intervals.  Excluding G1 and
re-referencing to $E_0=18\,\mathrm{MeV}$ gives
$r_{\rm foam,0}^{\rm max}=0.13$; the quasi-static bound remains
$\alpha\gtrsim0.65$ (unchanged) and the dynamical bound
$\alpha\gtrsim0.64$.
(ii)~All bounds assume $A_\alpha=1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi};
alternative normalizations shift $\alpha$ by
$\mathcal{O}(\ln A_\alpha/\ln(D_C/\ell_P))$.
The cosmological redshift correction of Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}
enhances $\sigma_\phi^2$ by $16\%$ at $z=0.151$, shifting the bound
by $\Delta\alpha\sim 5\times10^{-4}$ — negligible at the current
precision.
(iii)~The narrow-band condition $\sigma_\Delta\ll\tau_G$ underlying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_quadrature} is comfortably satisfied at the QS
bound but \emph{violated} at the DYN bound.  Using
$\sigma_\Delta=\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\ell/c$ (dynamical limit) and
$c\tau_G(E_0)=\hbar c/(r_{\rm int}E_0)\approx 4.8\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\sigma_\Delta}{\tau_G(E_0)} \;\approx\;
  \begin{cases}
    0.26 & \text{QS bound, $\sigma_\ell\approx 9\times 10^{-15}$ m,} \\
    2.47 & \text{DYN bound, $\sigma_\ell\approx 8.4\times 10^{-14}$ m.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
For the QS case the framework gives $\sigma_\Delta\to 0$ within the
relevant lag range (since $C_\alpha\to 1$ for $\tau<\tau_{\rm foam}$);
the narrow-band check above uses the dynamical-limit expression as an
upper bound and the QS bound itself follows from the phenomenological
identification Eq.~\eqref{eq:rfoam_qs} rather than from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss}.

For the DYN case the corrected bound sits in the
off-narrow-band regime $\sigma_\Delta\gtrsim\tau_G$, where the
carrier-included Eq.~\eqref{eq:g1obs_gauss} predicts \emph{two}
observable effects: (a)~line-width broadening
$r_{\rm obs}^2 = r_{\rm int}^2(1+\sigma_\Delta^2/\tau_G^2)$ --- which the
fit constrains via $r_{\rm foam,0}^{\rm max}=0.27$ --- and
(b)~a line-center redshift $\nu_0^{\rm eff}/\nu_0 =
\tau_G^2/(\tau_G^2+\sigma_\Delta^2)$ that becomes substantial at the
upper limit ($\nu_0^{\rm eff}/\nu_0\approx 0.14$ at
$\sigma_\Delta/\tau_G=2.47$).  The lever-arm fit uses observed line
centers as if they were intrinsic (pair-annihilation Doppler-boosted to
$E_i$ in each epoch) and does not separately constrain (b).
Incorporating the foam-induced redshift as a joint constraint would
generally tighten the bound, but requires an independent handle on the
source-frame line position (or on the per-epoch Lorentz factor) that is
not available in the present analysis.  The bound reported here is
therefore conservative; a joint center-plus-width fit is a natural
extension and would also test self-consistency of the dynamical-foam
interpretation at the upper limit.
(iv)~If the intrinsic fractional width $r_{\rm int}$ evolves with
energy (e.g.\ due to changing pair-plasma temperature across epochs),
this could partially mimic or mask a foam signal.  Marginalizing over a
free linear-in-$E$ nuisance term $r_{\rm int}(E) = r_0 + r_1(E/E_0)$
leaves both bounds essentially unchanged
($\alpha\gtrsim 0.64$--$0.65$), because the $E^2$ foam scaling has
distinct curvature from a linear intrinsic trend across the
factor-of-six energy range.  Numerically, the best-fit linear slope
$r_1$ is consistent with zero and, although the 95\%~CL upper limit
on $r_{\rm foam,0}$ broadens from $0.27$ to $0.31$ when the nuisance
is included, the corresponding $\alpha$ bound shifts by
$\Delta\alpha=\ln(0.31/0.27)/\ln(D_C/\ell_P)\approx 10^{-3}$ --- a
direct consequence of the logarithmic compression in the Ng--Perlman
relation --- confirming that the $E^2$ and linear-$E$ shapes are
effectively orthogonal for the seven-point lever-arm spanning
$9$--$37$~MeV.
(v)~The quasi-static and dynamical limits bracket the true foam
behavior; which regime applies depends on the (unknown) foam
correlation time $\tau_{\rm foam}$ relative to the source coherence
time $\tau_c\sim\hbar/\sigma_E\sim 2\times10^{-22}\,\mathrm{s}$ at
MeV energies.

\textit{Ravasio consistency check.}
Ravasio et al.~\cite{Ravasio2024} independently report
$\sigma/E = 1.31/12.56 \approx 10.4\%$
($\sigma_{\rm gauss}=1.31_{-0.31}^{+0.30}$\,MeV at
$E_{\rm line}=12.56\pm0.30$\,MeV) using Fermi/GBM data alone in the
$T+280$--$300$\,s interval, consistent with $r_{\rm obs}=0.10\pm0.01$
at $0.4\sigma$.  This cross-instrument agreement
(GECAM-C$+$Fermi/GBM versus Fermi/GBM alone) supports the
astrophysical reality of the constant fractional width.

Neither the quasi-static bound $\alpha\gtrsim0.65$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_grb_qs}) nor the dynamical bound
$\alpha\gtrsim0.64$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_grb_dyn}) excludes the
holographic value $\alpha=2/3\approx0.667$, but both improve upon the
single-line result $\alpha\gtrsim0.63$.
Reducing the per-epoch line-width uncertainties to $\delta r\lesssim
0.01$ --- achievable with future MeV spectroscopy missions --- would
push the quasi-static bound to $\alpha\gtrsim0.68$ and begin to
probe the holographic regime.

\subsection{CMB}
\label{sec:CMB}

For a broadband thermal source such as the CMB, foam does not introduce
a spectral-line shift but instead redistributes power: the coherent-core
component is suppressed by $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}$ and the missing flux
reappears in a broad pedestal whose shape depends on the short-time
behaviour of the correlation function $C_\alpha(\tau)$.  Because
$\sigma_\phi^2\propto\nu^2$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi}), the foam deficit
is concentrated at high frequencies while the pedestal fills in at low
frequencies, producing a spectral signature distinct from standard
chemical-potential ($\mu$) and Compton-$y$ distortions.  This subsection
sets up the broadband signal, derives it from first principles, analyses
the FIRAS sensitivity, and contrasts the result with the GRB
spectroscopic bound.

\subsubsection{Foam phase variance at CMB frequencies}
\label{sec:CMB_phasevar}

CMB photons travel from the last-scattering surface at comoving distance
$D_{\rm C}= 13.87\,{\rm Gpc}$ (Planck\,2018 flat-$\Lambda$CDM cosmology;
$H_0 = 67.36$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_m=0.315$).  The
accumulated phase variance, from Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi}, is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\phi^2(\nu)
    = \left(\frac{2\pi\nu}{c}\right)^{\!2}\!
      A_\alpha\,D_{\rm C}^{2(1-\alpha)}\,\ell_P^{2\alpha},
  \label{eq:sigma_phi_CMB}
\end{equation}
where $\nu$ is the observed frequency.  At $\nu=150$\,GHz,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\phi^2(150\,{\rm GHz})\approx
  \begin{cases}
    6.8\times10^{-2} & \alpha=1/2\ \text{(random walk)},\\
    10^{-22}         & \alpha=2/3\ \text{(holographic)}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:sigma_phi_CMB_values}
\end{equation}
The strong $\nu^2$ dependence concentrates foam effects at high
frequency; for $\alpha=1/2$ the variance reaches unity at
$\nu\approx 574$\,GHz, near the top of the FIRAS band, marking where the
linearised template breaks down (Section~\ref{sec:CMB_caveats}).  The
Fisher analysis of Section~\ref{sec:CMB_degeneracy} establishes the
observer-frame sensitivity threshold $\alpha\lesssim 0.57$ that follows
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma_phi_CMB}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi} treats foam variance as accumulating in
observer-frame comoving distance.  The proper redshift-weighted
integral Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi_cosmo} enhances $\sigma_\phi^2$ at
last scattering by a factor $\sim 10^4$ under uniform accumulation in
comoving distance (anchoring (a) of Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}),
corresponding to $\Delta\alpha\approx+0.033$ and tightening the
threshold to $\alpha\lesssim 0.60$.  Because the foam-evolution model in an
expanding universe is not specified by Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar}, we
treat the cosmological enhancement as a multiplicative modification of
$A_\alpha$ that the convention $A_\alpha=1$ absorbs.  We quote
$\alpha\lesssim 0.57$ as the observer-frame headline value; the
cosmological-accumulation variant $\alpha\lesssim 0.60$ represents the
tighter bound available if such an accumulation model is adopted.

\subsubsection{Foam-modified CMB spectrum}
\label{sec:CMB_derivation}

The narrow-band reduction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sobs_conv} --- which underlies
the spectral analysis of Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a} --- was derived for
quasi-monochromatic sources and does not apply to the broadband
blackbody.  We derive the broadband foam signature from the full
convolution integral in the fully dynamical limit $C_\alpha\to 0$.  In
this limit Eq.~\eqref{eq:diffVarExt} gives a saturated jitter variance
$\sigma_\Delta^2(\tau)\to 2\sigma_\ell^2(D)/c^2$ for all $\tau\neq 0$.

Decomposing the blackbody as an incoherent superposition of narrowband
modes $S_{\rm src}(\nu')=B_{\nu'}(T_0)$, each mode is modulated by the
\emph{same} achromatic jitter $\delta t(t)$ (geometric path-length
fluctuation, not plasma dispersion), with per-mode foam suppression
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau;\nu')
    = e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu')[1-C_\alpha(\tau)]},
  \quad
  \sigma_\phi^2(\nu')=(2\pi\nu'/c)^2\sigma_\ell^2(D),
\end{equation}
that approaches the constant $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu')}$ for all
$\tau\neq 0$ in the $C_\alpha\to 0$ limit.  To isolate the coherent-core
and pedestal contributions, we split
$\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau;\nu')=e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu')}+
[\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau;\nu')-e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu')}]$: the
first term contributes the constant asymptote at all lags
(Fourier-transforming to a delta function at $\nu=\nu'$), while the
residual is localised at $|\tau|\lesssim\tau_{\rm foam}$
(Fourier-transforming to a broad pedestal).  Taking the
intensity-weighted Fourier transform via Eq.~\eqref{eq:WK_obs} yields
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm obs}(\nu)
    = e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}\,B_\nu(T_0)
    + \int_0^\infty B_{\nu'}(T_0)\,\hat{R}(\nu-\nu';\nu')\,d\nu',
  \label{eq:CMB_spectrum}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{R}(\nu-\nu';\nu')$ is the Fourier transform of the residual
$\mathcal{F}_{\rm foam}(\tau;\nu')-e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu')}$.  The
coherent-core term $e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)}B_\nu(T_0)$ is exact and
model-independent (requiring only achromatic jitter and
$C_\alpha\to 0$); the pedestal kernel $\hat{R}$ depends on the
short-$\tau$ behaviour of $C_\alpha(\tau)$ --- the foam coherence
timescale $\tau_{\rm foam}$ --- and is therefore undetermined without
specifying $C_\alpha$.  This is the broadband generalisation of the
core-plus-pedestal decomposition stated in Section~\ref{sec:spec}.

The achromatic phase modulation $e^{i\omega\delta t(t)}$ at each
frequency is unitary, so it conserves photon number per mode: each
photon is merely time-shifted, not absorbed or created.  Any physical
pedestal kernel $\hat{R}$ derived from a specific $C_\alpha(\tau)$
therefore satisfies $\int [S_{\rm obs}(\nu)-B_\nu(T_0)]\,d\nu/(h\nu)=0$.
The fiducial placeholder used in the Fisher analysis below
($K_\nu^{\rm fast}\propto[\nu\ln(\nu_{\rm max}/\nu_{\rm min})]^{-1}$) is
energy-conserving but not photon-number-conserving; however, the
no-pedestal robustness check (Section~\ref{sec:CMB_degeneracy}) bounds
the impact of the kernel choice to $\Delta\alpha\approx 0.009$, so the
headline threshold is insensitive to this violation at the stated
precision.  Replacing the placeholder by a physical kernel from a
specified $C_\alpha$ model is a prerequisite for a definitive FIRAS
bound.

\subsubsection{Fast-foam validity}
\label{sec:CMB_fastfoam}

The above derivation requires $\tau_{\rm foam}\ll 1/\nu_{\rm CMB}\sim
7$\,ps.  For Planck-scale foam, $\tau_{\rm foam}\sim\ell_P/c\approx
5\times 10^{-44}$\,s, the condition is satisfied by more than thirty orders of
magnitude.  For larger $\tau_{\rm foam}$ the suppression factor
$e^{-\sigma_\phi^2[1-C_\alpha(\tau_{\rm foam})]}$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ffoam}) is reduced by $[1-C_\alpha(\tau_{\rm foam})]$
and the distortion amplitude is suppressed accordingly.  In the
quasi-static limit $\tau_{\rm foam}\to\infty$, $C_\alpha\to 1$ and both
the deficit and the pedestal vanish, consistent with the disappearance
of the HBT Siegert signal in that limit (Section~\ref{sec:g1}).

\subsubsection{Fisher analysis: degeneracy with $\mu$ and $y$}
\label{sec:CMB_degeneracy}

We use $I_\nu\equiv S_{\rm obs}(\nu)$ to denote the observed CMB
intensity including the foam signal.
The COBE/FIRAS monopole spectrum over $60$--$600$\,GHz constrains
spectral distortions at $|\mu|,\,|y|\lesssim 10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$
\cite{Fixsen1996}.  Working in the linearised regime
$\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)\ll 1$, we treat four spectral parameters
$\boldsymbol{\theta}=(A_{\rm eff},T_0,\mu,y)$, where
$A_{\rm eff}=A_\alpha D_C^{2(1-\alpha)}\ell_P^{2\alpha}=\sigma_\ell^2(D_C)$
is the cumulative foam path-length variance at the CMB distance
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:NgPerlmanVar}), so that
$\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)=(2\pi\nu/c)^2 A_{\rm eff}$.  The
foam template is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial I_\nu}{\partial A_{\rm eff}}
    = -\!\left(\frac{2\pi\nu}{c}\right)^{\!2} B_\nu(T_0)
      + K_\nu^{\rm fast}\!\int_{\nu_{\rm min}}^{\nu_{\rm max}}
        \left(\frac{2\pi\nu'}{c}\right)^{\!2} B_{\nu'}(T_0)\,d\nu',
  \label{eq:dIdA}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:dIdA} comprises a high-frequency deficit and a
compensating pedestal $K_\nu^{\rm fast}$.  Adopting the placeholder
$K_\nu^{\rm fast}\propto(\nu\ln[\nu_{\rm max}/\nu_{\rm min}])^{-1}$
(uniform energy redistribution in $\ln\nu$ --- the simplest form
consistent with energy conservation) makes the pedestal integral
$P_{\rm foam}\equiv\int(2\pi\nu'/c)^2 B_{\nu'}(T_0)\,d\nu'$ a
frequency-independent constant, yielding a $1/\nu$ pedestal --- the
same Rayleigh--Jeans shape as the $\mu$-distortion template, and hence
the origin of the foam--$\mu$ degeneracy.

The chemical-potential and Compton-$y$ distortion templates are
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial I_\nu}{\partial\mu}
    &= -B_\nu(T_0)\,\frac{x e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}, \label{eq:dIdmu}\\
  \frac{\partial I_\nu}{\partial y}
    &= \phantom{-}B_\nu(T_0)\,\frac{x e^x}{e^x-1}
       \!\left[\frac{x(e^x+1)}{e^x-1}-4\right], \label{eq:dIdy}
\end{align}
with $x=h\nu/(k_B T_0)$, encoding respectively early-universe energy
injection (Silk damping, decaying particles) and late-time electron
heating (reionization, cluster gas).  In the Rayleigh--Jeans limit the
$\mu$-template approaches $\Delta I/B\to -\mu/x\propto\nu^{-1}$, the
same frequency dependence as the fiducial pedestal $K_\nu^{\rm fast}$;
the two templates therefore have nearly identical spectral shapes
across the FIRAS band, producing a strong Fisher-matrix degeneracy.

A Fisher analysis using 43 uniform FIRAS channels with diagonal noise
$\sigma_\nu=0.01$\,MJy/sr yields the correlation matrix for parameters
$(A_{\rm eff},\,T_0,\,\mu,\,y)$ in that order:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix}
  1.000 & 0.937 & \mathbf{0.976} & 0.010 \\
  0.937 & 1.000 & 0.969 & -0.212 \\
  \mathbf{0.976} & 0.969 & 1.000 & -0.127 \\
  0.010 & -0.212 & -0.127 & 1.000
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:fisher_corr_matrix}
\end{equation}
showing the dominant foam--$\mu$ degeneracy
($\rho_{A_{\rm eff}\mu}=0.976$, bold) and a negligible foam--$y$
correlation ($\rho_{A_{\rm eff}y}=0.010$).  The marginal Fisher
uncertainties are
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(A_{\rm eff}) = 1.5\times10^{-17}\,{\rm m}^2,
  \qquad
  \sigma(A_{\rm eff})\big|_{\mu,y\,\text{fixed}}
    = 2.6\times10^{-18}\,{\rm m}^2,
  \label{eq:sigma_Aeff}
\end{equation}
so marginalising over $\mu$ and $T_0$ degrades the foam-amplitude
uncertainty by a factor $\sim 6$ (the full $4\times4$ marginal factor
exceeds the naive two-parameter estimate
$(1-\rho_{A\mu}^2)^{-1/2}\approx 4.6$ because of the additional $T_0$
degeneracy $\rho_{AT_0}=0.937$), driven almost entirely by the
foam--$\mu$ collinearity.  Figure~\ref{fig:cmb_snr} plots the SNR
versus $\alpha$; setting $A_{\rm eff}^{\rm Ng\text{-}Perlman}(\alpha,D_C)
=\sigma(A_{\rm eff})$ and solving for $\alpha$ yields the threshold
$\alpha\lesssim 0.57$.

We have also checked the no-pedestal limit, appropriate when
$\tau_{\rm foam}\sim\ell_P/c$ displaces the redistributed power to
$\sim 10^{43}$\,Hz, entirely outside the FIRAS band: the foam template
then degenerates with $\partial B_\nu/\partial T_0$ at
$\rho\approx 0.9997$ and the sensitivity worsens by $N\sim 13\times$,
yet $\alpha_{\rm threshold}$ shifts only to $\sim 0.56$.  The
near-coincidence reflects the logarithmic Ng--Perlman relation
$\sigma_\ell^2=D_C^{2(1-\alpha)}\ell_P^{2\alpha}$: taking the
$\alpha$-derivative of $\ln\sigma_\ell^2 = 2(1-\alpha)\ln D_C +
2\alpha\ln\ell_P$ at fixed $\sigma_\ell$ gives $d\alpha/d\ln\sigma_\ell
= 1/\ln(\ell_P/D_C)$, so a factor-$N$ worsening in sensitivity shifts
$\alpha$ by only $\Delta\alpha=\ln N/[2\ln(D_C/\ell_P)]
\approx 0.009$ for $N\approx 13$.
The threshold $\alpha\lesssim 0.57$ is therefore robust to the kernel
model at this precision; a definitive analysis requires specifying
$C_\alpha(\tau)$ from first principles to determine the physical
pedestal shape within the FIRAS band.

\subsubsection{Bias in recovered $T_0$ from unmodeled foam}
\label{sec:CMB_T0_bias}

The Fisher matrix also quantifies how much the fitted CMB temperature
would shift if foam were present but omitted from the model.  Suppose
the data contain foam at amplitude $A_{\rm eff}^{\rm true}$ but the
analyst fits only $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm fit}=(T_0,\mu,y)$.  In the
linearised Fisher regime the parameter shift is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\rm fit}
    - \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm fit}^{\rm true}
  = -(F_{\rm fit,fit})^{-1}\,F_{\rm fit,omit}\,A_{\rm eff}^{\rm true},
  \label{eq:bias_formula}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm fit,fit}$ is the $3\times3$ Fisher block for
$(T_0,\mu,y)$ and $F_{\rm fit,omit}$ couples the fitted parameters to
the omitted foam amplitude.  Both blocks come from the same matrix as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fisher_corr_matrix}, so no new computation is required.

The $T_0$ component gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta T_0 = b_{T_0}\,A_{\rm eff}^{\rm true},
  \qquad b_{T_0} = -\bigl[(F_{\rm fit,fit})^{-1}F_{\rm fit,omit}\bigr]_{T_0}.
  \label{eq:T0_bias}
\end{equation}
Numerically, $b_{T_0}=+1.21\times10^7\,{\rm K\,m^{-2}}$: the bias is
\emph{positive}, reflecting the placeholder pedestal's Rayleigh--Jeans
excess being absorbed as an apparent temperature increase
($\partial B_\nu/\partial T>0$), while the high-frequency deficit is
absorbed into a positive $\Delta\mu$ ($b_\mu\approx 1.05\times 10^8\,
{\rm m^{-2}}$, consistent with $\rho_{A_{\rm eff}\mu}=0.976$).
Requiring $|\Delta T_0|<\sigma(T_0)=5.7\times 10^{-4}$\,K
\cite{Fixsen2009} gives
\begin{equation}
  A_{\rm eff}^{\rm max,\,bias}
    = \frac{\sigma(T_0)}{|b_{T_0}|} = 4.7\times 10^{-11}\,{\rm m}^2,
  \label{eq:Aeff_bias_bound}
\end{equation}
corresponding (with $A_\alpha=1$) to a crossover exponent
$\alpha_{\rm bias}=0.518$.  Models with $\alpha<0.518$ would have
biased the Fixsen $T_0$ by more than $1\sigma$ had such a signal been
present and unmodeled; models with $\alpha>0.518$ would not.

The crossover $\alpha\approx 0.52$ marks the boundary below which
unmodeled foam would bias $T_0$ by more than $1\sigma(T_0)$; since this
is below the detection threshold $\alpha\lesssim 0.57$, the temperature
measurement is unbiased for any foam model near or above the SNR
threshold.  (This should be contrasted with the GRB lower exclusion
bound $\alpha\gtrsim 0.64$: the two instruments probe complementary
regimes of the $\alpha$ parameter space---FIRAS sets an upper
detectability limit while the GRB analysis sets a lower exclusion
bound.)  Mechanistically, the foam pedestal's Rayleigh--Jeans-like
low-frequency excess overlaps the $\mu$-distortion template much more
than the $\partial B_\nu/\partial T_0$ template, so in the
four-parameter fit the foam signal is absorbed primarily into $\mu$,
leaving $T_0$ nearly unshifted (small bias).  That same $\mu$-direction
degeneracy, however, inflates the marginal $\sigma(A_{\rm eff})$ once
$\mu$ is allowed to float, sharpening the SNR threshold relative to the
bias threshold.
Figure~\ref{fig:cmb_bias} plots $|\Delta T_0|/\sigma(T_0)$ as a
function of $\alpha$; at $\alpha=0.57$ (marginal SNR $\approx 1$) the
ratio is $\approx 4\times 10^{-4}$ --- completely negligible.  The
published Fixsen $T_0=2.72548$\,K is therefore not appreciably biased
by any foam model near or above the FIRAS detection threshold.  The
sign and magnitude of $\Delta T_0$ depend on the placeholder kernel;
a physically derived pedestal from a specified $C_\alpha(\tau)$ may
alter these values.

\subsubsection{Caveats: nonlinear regime and parameter degeneracy}
\label{sec:CMB_caveats}

Two caveats qualify the linearised analysis.

\emph{Nonlinear regime.}  The linearisation
$\Delta I_\nu\approx\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)\times[\text{template}]$ is valid
only for $\sigma_\phi^2\ll 1$.  For $\alpha=1/2$, $\sigma_\phi^2=1$ is
reached at $\nu\approx 574$\,GHz, within the FIRAS band.  Replacing
the linearised deficit $-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)B_\nu$ by the saturated form
$-(1-e^{-\sigma_\phi^2(\nu)})B_\nu$ reduces the \emph{integrated}
in-band foam amplitude (summed over FIRAS channels) by $\approx 13\%$
at $\alpha=0.50$ and by $<1\%$ at $\alpha\gtrsim 0.52$.
The headline threshold $\alpha\lesssim 0.57$ is therefore unaffected
at the $\Delta\alpha\ll 10^{-3}$ level; the nonlinear template matters
only for analyses of strong foam ($\alpha\lesssim 0.52$), where it may
also help break the foam--$\mu$ degeneracy.

\emph{Parameter degeneracy within the foam template.}  The exponent
$\alpha$ and the amplitude $A_\alpha$ enter only through the product
$A_\alpha D_C^{2(1-\alpha)}\ell_P^{2\alpha}=A_{\rm eff}$.  FIRAS
constrains this product directly but not the exponent separately;
converting a bound on $A_{\rm eff}$ to a bound on $\alpha$ requires
fixing $A_\alpha$ by convention (here $A_\alpha=1$, following Ng \&
Perlman~\cite{NgPerlman2022}).  Any quoted bound on $\alpha$ carries
this implicit normalisation.

\subsubsection{Complementarity with GRB\,221009A}
\label{sec:CMB_complementarity}

The FIRAS sensitivity range $\alpha\lesssim 0.57$--$0.60$ does not
overlap the GRB spectroscopic lower bound $\alpha\gtrsim 0.64$ of
Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a}: FIRAS probes strictly lower exponents.
Any foam model consistent with the GRB bound has $\sigma_\phi^2\ll 1$
at CMB frequencies, so the $T_0$ bias is entirely negligible
(Section~\ref{sec:CMB_T0_bias} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cmb_bias}).

FIRAS provides an independent broadband test of foam --- absolute
calibrated photometry, no source-profile assumption, and a different
systematic-error budget from spectroscopy --- and constrains the
parameter space below the GRB bound where more exotic foam models
would reside.  A definitive FIRAS foam analysis would require
specifying $C_\alpha(\tau)$ to determine the physical pedestal shape
and replacing the linearised template with the full nonlinear
expression at high frequencies where $\sigma_\phi^2\gtrsim 1$;
Appendix~\ref{app:FIRAS} outlines the steps such an analysis would
take.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

All single-source spectroscopic bounds derived in this paper assume the
dynamical-foam limit $\tau_{\rm foam}\ll\tau_c$
(Section~\ref{sec:closedforms}), in which the foam path-length jitter
decorrelates many times within each photon coherence time.  If foam
fluctuations are instead slow ($\tau_{\rm foam}\gg\tau_c$), the
accumulated phase is essentially frozen during each coherence time and
the foam acts as a deterministic, frequency-independent phase screen
--- no spectral broadening or peak reduction occurs within a single
observation, and the shape-distortion bounds do not apply.  The CMB
constraint (Section~\ref{sec:CMB}), which relies on absolute
spectrophotometry against a known blackbody spectrum rather than on line
shape, remains valid independently of $\tau_{\rm foam}$.

A principal instrumental limitation of the present analysis is the MeV
sensitivity gap: the spectral region between the upper edge of the
Fermi-GBM BGO detector response ($\lesssim40\,\mathrm{MeV}$, where the
effective area falls steeply) and the regime in which Fermi-LAT achieves
robust detection efficiency ($\gtrsim100\,\mathrm{MeV}$).
The frequency $\nu_\star \simeq 37\,\mathrm{MeV}$ at which the foam-induced
spectral break is predicted to be most constraining falls squarely within
this gap, so neither instrument provides fully resolved coverage across
the critical transition.
Closing the MeV gap is a recognized community priority, addressed by
next-generation satellite concepts that combine Compton-scattering and
pair-conversion detection techniques to achieve continuous sensitivity from
$\sim100\,\mathrm{keV}$ to beyond $1\,\mathrm{GeV}$.
The All-sky Medium Energy Gamma-ray Observatory
(AMEGO~\cite{McEnery2019}) and its MIDEX-scale variant
AMEGO-X~\cite{Caputo2022} are designed to cover $100\,\mathrm{keV}$ to
$1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ with order-of-magnitude improvements over both COMPTEL
and Fermi-LAT in the MeV decade; the ESA-proposed M-class concept
e-ASTROGAM~\cite{DeAngelis2017} targets a comparable bandpass.
The near-term NASA SMEX mission COSI (scheduled launch $\sim$2027)
will extend broadband sensitivity to $0.2$--$5\,\mathrm{MeV}$, providing
a valuable pathfinder, though its upper energy bound falls well short of
the $37\,\mathrm{MeV}$ regime and the gap therefore remains open.
Realization of any of the broader-bandpass concepts would, for the first
time, permit fully resolved spectral measurements of GRB prompt emission
across the $37\,\mathrm{MeV}$ transition without the instrumental
discontinuity that limits the present work, removing a major systematic
and enabling substantially tighter tests of spacetime-foam models.

The proposed PIXIE satellite~\cite{Kogut2011} --- a fully cryogenic
($T_{\rm sys}\approx2.7$\,K) nulling polarizing Fourier transform spectrometer
spanning $30$\,GHz to $6$\,THz --- is projected to surpass FIRAS sensitivity
by roughly three orders of magnitude, reaching $|\mu|\lesssim10^{-8}$ and
$|y|\lesssim10^{-9}$ and entering the regime of guaranteed $\Lambda$CDM
spectral signals from Silk damping and reionization.
Propagating this gain through the Fisher-matrix scaling derived in
Section~\ref{sec:CMB}, the marginal foam detection threshold shifts from
$\alpha\lesssim0.57$ (FIRAS) to approximately $\alpha\lesssim0.60$ (PIXIE),
narrowing the gap with the spectroscopic lower
bound $\alpha\gtrsim0.64$ from GRB\,221009A (Fig.~\ref{fig:cmb_snr}).
Both FIRAS and PIXIE thresholds are quoted for the placeholder
$K_\nu^{\rm fast}\propto 1/\nu$ pedestal kernel and the observer-frame
$\sigma_\phi^2$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi}; a physically derived
$C_\alpha(\tau)$ would generally relax the foam--$\mu$ degeneracy and
tighten these bounds, while the cosmological-redshift weighting of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaphi_cosmo} could shift each by up to
$\Delta\alpha\sim 0.03$ in the same direction.
The dominant foam--$\mu$ degeneracy ($\rho\approx0.98$) persists on the
linearized template, but PIXIE's extension to $6$\,THz accesses the nonlinear
regime $\sigma_\phi^2\gg1$ for $\alpha\lesssim0.55$, where the foam template
diverges markedly from the $\mu$-distortion shape and the degeneracy may be
substantially reduced.  The $\alpha\lesssim0.60$ projection is therefore
conservative: if the nonlinear template effectively breaks the foam--$\mu$
degeneracy at PIXIE sensitivity, the true threshold could be significantly
more stringent.
Complementary concepts including PRISM~\cite{Andre2014} and BISOU would push
sensitivity further; a generation of absolute spectrophotometry missions thus
opens a distinct window onto foam parameter space below the spectroscopic
bound established in Section~\ref{sec:grb221009a}.

\section{Outline of a Full FIRAS Foam Analysis}
\label{app:FIRAS}
% ==============================================================

The Fisher and bias analyses of Section~\ref{sec:CMB} use a placeholder
pedestal kernel and a linearised template; a definitive FIRAS bound on
the foam exponent $\alpha$ requires the additional steps outlined
here.  The load-bearing derivations, numerical results, and
complementarity discussion now live in Section~\ref{sec:CMB}.

\subsection{Proposed Analysis}
\label{sec:FIRAS_analysis}

The FIRAS constraint requires the following steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Specify $C_\alpha(\tau)$ model.} Choose or derive the
  temporal decorrelation function (e.g.\ exponential with timescale
  $\tau_{\rm foam}$, power-law, or data-driven).  Use
  Section~\ref{sec:CMB_derivation} to compute the pedestal kernel
  $\hat{R}$ from first principles.

\item \textit{Compute nonlinear Fisher matrix.} Using the full
  nonlinear template (not the linearised approximation), compute the
  Fisher correlation matrix for $(\alpha, A_\alpha, T_0, \mu, y,
  A_{\rm CIB}, c_i)$ on the FIRAS noise-plus-calibration covariance.
  Assess how much the $\alpha$--$\mu$ degeneracy is reduced once
  $C_\alpha$ is specified.

\item[2b.] \textit{Repeat the bias calculation in the nonlinear regime.}
  Once the full nonlinear template is available, repeat the linearised
  bias computation of Section~\ref{sec:CMB_T0_bias} at the nonlinear
  level to verify that the sign and magnitude of $\Delta T_0$ are
  reliable at the band edge ($\nu\gtrsim 400$\,GHz, where
  $\sigma_\phi^2\to 1$ for $\alpha\approx 0.50$--$0.55$).  In the
  nonlinear regime the linearised formula
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias_formula} must be replaced by a numerical
  comparison of maximum-likelihood estimators.

\item \textit{Null-hypothesis validation.} Fit the FIRAS spectrum with
  $A_\alpha=0$ and verify recovery of published values:
  $T_0 = 2.72548\pm 0.00057$\,K, $|\mu|<9\times 10^{-5}$,
  $|y|<1.5\times 10^{-5}$ (95\% CL; Fixsen 1996, 2009).

\item \textit{Joint Bayesian inference.} Sample the full posterior
  $p(\alpha, A_\alpha, T_0, \mu, y, \ldots \mid \mathbf{I}^{\rm obs})$
  using the full nonlinear likelihood and FIRAS data.  Report marginal
  bounds on $\alpha$ (fixing $A_\alpha=1$ or marginalising over it as
  specified).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Likelihood and Inference Framework}

The FIRAS monopole spectrum is compared to the full foam template
(Section~\ref{sec:CMB_derivation}) together with standard
spectral-distortion templates ($\mu$-distortion, Compton-$y$, CIB, and
calibration modes) via a Gaussian log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
  \ln\mathcal{L}
    = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{r}^T\,\mathbf{C}^{-1}\,\mathbf{r},
  \label{eq:FIRAS_likelihood}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{I}^{\rm obs} - \mathbf{I}^{\rm model}$ is
the residual vector and $\mathbf{C}$ is the FIRAS
noise-plus-calibration covariance matrix (Fixsen 1996).  The free
parameters are $(\alpha, A_\alpha, T_0, \mu, y, A_{\rm CIB},
c_1, c_2, c_3)$, where $c_i$ are calibration mode amplitudes.  A
null-hypothesis validation fit with $A_\alpha = 0$ must recover the
published FIRAS values $T_0 = 2.72548\pm 0.00057$\,K,
$|\mu|<9\times 10^{-5}$, $|y|<1.5\times 10^{-5}$ (95\% CL) before the
foam parameter is freed for analysis.
